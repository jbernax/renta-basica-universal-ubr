# M11 · Political economy, public opinion, and coalition feasibility

## 1. Why this matters
Even an economically optimal and constitutionally valid UBR plan will fail if it cannot assemble a durable legislative coalition in Spain's fragmented bicameral parliament (*Congreso de los Diputados* and *Senado*), secure buy-in from social partners (trade unions CCOO and UGT, employer confederations CEOE and CEPYME), and survive autonomous-community veto politics—as demonstrated by the 2023 parliamentary blockage of the Catalan UBR pilot budget [@van-parijs-vanderborght-2017; @tsebelis-2002-veto-players]. In Spain's institutional architecture, the Senate wields an absolute veto over fiscal stability targets and spending ceilings under *Ley Orgánica 2/2012* (LOEPSF Art. 15 [@boe-loepsf-2012]), making bicameral veto-player alignment mandatory.

## 2. Research questions
- **RQ-M11-01**: How do policy design attributes—framing (traditional "Universal Basic Income" vs. "AI Productivity Dividend" vs. "Refundable IRPF Tax Credit"), financing source (labor IRPF vs. AI/corporate/wealth levies `M16`), residency rules (`M13`), and benefit level—shift support among Spanish median-decile voters and across party electorates, evaluated through the CARIN deservingness heuristic [@van-oorschot-2000-deservingness]?
  - *Hypothesis:* In a pre-registered conjoint experiment on a representative Spanish sample ($N = 3,500$), framing UBR as an IRPF-integrated "AI & Automation Dividend" financed predominantly by corporate/AI/capital levies (`M16`) with a 2–3 year residency requirement increases majority support by +14 to +20 percentage points compared to a labor-tax-financed welfare framing. By attributing income losses to exogenous technological dislocation rather than individual effort, the AI framing drastically lowers perceived recipient "Control" failure under the CARIN criteria (Control, Attitude, Reciprocity, Identity, Need [@van-oorschot-2000-deservingness]), securing broad cross-ideological backing.
- **RQ-M11-02**: Under what institutional bargains do Spanish social partners (trade unions CCOO, UGT; employer confederations CEOE, CEPYME) and regionalist swing caucuses (PNV, Junts, Bildu) endorse UBR, and how can bicameral veto points (Senate LOEPSF Art. 15.6) be navigated through organic legislative reform?
  - *Hypothesis:* Social partner endorsement requires a tripartite grand bargain under Art. 7 CE and the CES (*Ley 21/1991*): for unions, (i) strict statutory non-supplantation clauses preventing employers from citing UBR to depress bargained collective wages or statutory SMI hikes [@vanderborght-2006-trade-unions; @vlandas-2019-politics-ubi], (ii) preservation of the `M07` contributory pension accrual under Art. 273 TRLGSS, and (iii) social partner co-governance of AI transition funds (`M16`); for employers (CEOE/CEPYME), statutory certainty on maximum corporate tax rates, partial exemptions for SME reinvested surplus, and wage flexibility under Art. 82.3 ET *descuelgues salariales*. For bicameral passage, enacting an Organic Law amending LOEPSF Art. 15.6 by absolute majority in Congress restores the general constitutional override rule (Art. 90.2 CE, STC 179/1989 [@stc-179-1989]), stripping the Senate of its absolute veto over stability targets.

## 3. State of knowledge
Comparative political economy shows that universal programs enjoy greater long-term fiscal resilience than narrowly targeted means-tested programs ("paradox of redistribution"), yet face high initial enactment hurdles because visible gross tax hikes alarm middle-decile voters and insiders [@van-parijs-vanderborght-2017; @hoynes-rothstein-2019]. Tsebelis's [@tsebelis-2002-veto-players] veto-player framework demonstrates that multi-arena systems with territorial upper chambers require broad consensual policy packages. Political studies of basic income confirm that union skepticism stems from fears of wage suppression and welfare-state retrenchment [@vanderborght-2006-trade-unions; @vlandas-2019-politics-ubi], while employer resistance centers on labor supply withdrawal and corporate tax burdens. Public opinion research reveals that social solidarity is governed by the CARIN deservingness criteria [@van-oorschot-2000-deservingness], wherein public generosity depends critically on perceived lack of recipient control over distress, identity affinity, and civic reciprocity.

## 4. Spain context & data sources
- **Public Opinion Data:** *Centro de Investigaciones Sociológicas* (CIS) barometers, European Social Survey (ESS) Spanish waves on welfare attitudes, and CEO (*Centre d'Estudis d'Opinió*) surveys on the Catalan UBR pilot.
- **Institutional Actors & Legislation:** Parliamentary records (*Diario de Sesiones del Congreso y Senado* on IMV [@boe-imv-2021]), *Ley Orgánica 2/2012 de Estabilidad Presupuestaria y Sostenibilidad Financiera* (LOEPSF [@boe-loepsf-2012]), jurisprudence of the Constitutional Court on legislative preeminence of the lower house (STC 179/1989 [@stc-179-1989]), and official position papers of CCOO, UGT, CEOE-CEPYME, and autonomous-community governments.

## 5. Methods & identification
1. **Pre-Registered Conjoint Survey Experiment ($N = 3,500$ Spanish Voters):** Randomize 6 policy dimensions (Benefit Amount, Tax Financing Mechanism, AI Trigger Condition, Residency Rule, Treatment of Existing Pensions/Benefits, Administrative Level) to estimate Average Marginal Component Effects (AMCEs) by income decile, CNO-11 AI-exposure risk (`M01`), autonomous community, and party vote recall. Explicitly evaluate the 5 CARIN dimensions [@van-oorschot-2000-deservingness] via vignette-based rating batteries.
2. **Bicameral Veto-Player & Coalition Mapping:** Embed Tsebelis's [@tsebelis-2002-veto-players] spatial equilibrium model across both the *Congreso de los Diputados* and the *Senado*, evaluating spatial distance and win-sets. Specify the organic legislative strategy in Congress to amend LOEPSF Art. 15.6 by absolute majority to restore the constitutional override rule (Art. 90.2 CE, STC 179/1989 [@stc-179-1989]), neutralizing Senate obstructionism.
3. **Tripartite Social Dialogue Formulation:** Structure the institutional bargaining table under Art. 7 CE between the Government, trade unions (CCOO, UGT), and business confederations (CEOE, CEPYME), balancing non-supplantation guarantees with enterprise productivity incentives.

## 6. Pilot implications
- **Municipal Tier:** Feasible under local council municipal decrees (*ordenanzas municipales*), characterized by lower legislative veto hurdles but high vulnerability to local electoral cycles and administrative discontinuation (as observed post-B-MINCOME in Barcelona [@todeschini-bmincome-2020]).
- **Regional Tier (*Régimen Común* vs. *Régimen Foral*):**
  - In *Régimen Común* (e.g. Catalonia), pilots require regional parliamentary budget authorization, leaving them exposed to cross-party ideological vetoes and legal challenges from the central government regarding State jurisdictional competencies (`M06`).
  - In *Régimen Foral* (Basque Country and Navarre), established bipartisan regional majorities and complete fiscal-legislative autonomy under the *Concierto Económico* offer the highest institutional stability for a multi-year experimental pilot.
- **National Tier:** Requires navigating the bicameral veto architecture: passing ordinary enabling legislation in Congress paired with an Organic Law reforming LOEPSF Art. 15.6 to overcome the Senate's absolute veto [@boe-loepsf-2012; @stc-179-1989], anchored in a formal Tripartite Social Dialogue Agreement.

## 7. Risks, counterarguments & open issues
- **Counterargument (Territorial Fiscal Backlash):** Net-contributing regions with higher nominal wages and cost of living (Madrid, Catalonia, Balearics) will show a higher share of net IRPF contributors to a uniform national UBR (`M09`). *Mitigation:* Quantify inter-regional fiscal flows transparently and test whether pairing national Tier A with regionally financed cost-of-living supplements (`M13`) stabilizes territorial coalitions.
- **Counterargument (Employer Investment Flight):** Business confederations (CEOE/CEPYME) argue that UBR financing raises the user cost of capital and induces capital flight. *Mitigation:* Demonstrate that destination-based AI compute withholding (`M16`) and SME surplus reinvestment deductions preserve domestic operating capital.
