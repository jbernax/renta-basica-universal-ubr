# M06 · Constitutional, competence, and EU legal architecture

## 1. Why this matters
Any Spanish UBR proposal will face immediate constitutional review before the *Tribunal Constitucional* (TC) over the distribution of powers between the State and the 17 *Comunidades Autónomas*, as well as scrutiny under European Union free-movement, non-discrimination, and social-security coordination law [@boe-ce-1978; @eu-reg-883-2004; @eu-dir-2004-38-free-movement]. Furthermore, Spain's asymmetric fiscal federalism—split between the *régimen común* (*LOFCA* [@boe-lofca-1980], *Ley 22/2009* [@boe-ley-financiacion-ccaa-2009]) and the *régimen foral* (Basque *Concierto Económico* [@boe-concierto-vasco-2002] and Navarre *Convenio* [@boe-convenio-navarra-1990])—requires distinct statutory harmonization instruments.

## 2. Research questions
- **RQ-M06-01**: Can a national UBR be enacted in Spain via ordinary State legislation (*Ley Ordinaria* + *Ley Orgánica* coordinating LOFCA) anchored in Articles 149.1.1, 149.1.14, 149.1.17, and 41 of the Spanish Constitution without requiring constitutional reform across scenarios S1–S3?
  - *Hypothesis:* Yes—structuring UBR as a dual instrument combining a refundable individual IRPF credit (State competence over *Hacienda general*, Art. 149.1.14 CE [@boe-ce-1978]) with a non-contributory Social Security floor (Arts. 41 and 149.1.17 CE, validated by TC doctrine in STC 158/2021 [@tc-stc-158-2021-imv] and STC 19/2024 [@tc-stc-19-2024-imv-foral]) is fully constitutional without amending the 1978 Constitution, transitioning under S3 to public economic initiative (Art. 128.2 CE) for sovereign fund dividend disbursement (`M16`).
- **RQ-M06-02**: Under European Union law (Art. 45 TFEU, Directive 2004/38/EC [@eu-dir-2004-38-free-movement], and Regulation 883/2004 [@eu-reg-883-2004]), what legal architecture prevents mandatory cross-border exportability abroad while respecting non-discrimination for EU workers?
  - *Hypothesis:* Because an unconditional universal transfer cannot legally be sheltered as an Annex X "special non-contributory cash benefit" under Regulation 883/2004, a **Bifurcated EU-Law Architecture**—granting immediate pro-rata IRPF tax-credit integration to economically active EU workers/self-employed under Art. 45 TFEU and Directive 2004/38/EC Art. 24(1), while conditioning non-contributory cash entitlement for economically inactive EU citizens and third-country entrants on lawful, habitual residency under Directive 2004/38/EC Art. 24(2) and *Ley Orgánica 4/2000* [@boe-extranjeria-2000]—is fully defensible before the CJEU.

## 3. State of knowledge
Spanish constitutional scholarship and landmark *Tribunal Constitucional* judgments on the *Ingreso Mínimo Vital*—specifically STC 158/2021 [@tc-stc-158-2021-imv] (upholding State legislative competence under Arts. 149.1.1 and 149.1.17 CE) and STC 19/2024 [@tc-stc-19-2024-imv-foral] (validating the transfer of administrative management to the Basque Country and Navarre)—confirm that while Autonomous Communities hold exclusive competence over regional *asistencia social* (Art. 148.1.20 CE), the State holds exclusive power to establish basic economic floors and tax-transfer integration [@boe-ce-1978]. Under EU law, CJEU jurisprudence (*Dano*, *Alimanovic*, *Commission v UK*) establishes that Member States may condition non-contributory social assistance for economically inactive Union citizens on lawful residency, whereas economically active workers are protected by strict equal treatment.

## 5. Methods & identification
1. **Statutory Reform Drafting Blueprint:** Draft explicit amendment clauses for (i) *Ley 35/2006* (introducing the individual refundable UBR tax credit and repealing Arts. 20, 56–61, 84), (ii) *TRLGSS* (establishing the non-contributory universal base while preserving contributory slopes `M07`), and (iii) *Ley 22/2009* [@boe-ley-financiacion-ccaa-2009] (adjusting the 50% ceded regional IRPF tranche to safeguard regional fiscal autonomy).
2. **Constitutional Scenario Mapping (S1–S3):** Map statutory anchors across scenarios: S1 relies on Arts. 149.1.14 and 149.1.17 CE; S2 utilizes LOFCA Art. 157.3 CE rebalancing as labor IRPF erodes; and S3 invokes Art. 128.2 CE (public initiative in essential resources) for sovereign compute/AI dividend fund governance (`M16`).

## 6. Pilot implications
- **Municipal Tier:** Grounded in Art. 25.2.e of *Ley 7/1985 Reguladora de las Bases del Régimen Local* [@boe-lbrl-1985] (municipal social action). Requires an explicit State statutory tax exemption in the *Ley de Presupuestos Generales del Estado* so pilot transfers are not taxed under general IRPF or counted against existing IMV eligibility.
- **Regional Tier (*Régimen Común* vs. *Régimen Foral*):** In *Régimen Común*, pilot implementation requires State authorization to adjust the regional IRPF scale under *Ley 22/2009*; in *Régimen Foral* (Basque Country / Navarre), the *Diputaciones Forales* possess full sovereign competence under the *Concierto Económico* [@boe-concierto-vasco-2002] to legislate both the transfer and the regional IRPF withholding tariff directly.
- **National Tier:** State *Ley Ordinaria* enacted by the *Cortes Generales*, coordinated with the *Comisión Mixta del Concierto Económico*.

## 7. Risks, counterarguments & open issues
- **Counterargument (Infringement of EU Free Movement):** Imposing a 2-year residency requirement on inactive EU citizens could face European Commission infringement proceedings. *Mitigation:* Structure the requirement strictly in accordance with Article 24(2) of Directive 2004/38/EC, and grant immediate pro-rata tax credit status to all contributing workers under Art. 45 TFEU (`M13`).
